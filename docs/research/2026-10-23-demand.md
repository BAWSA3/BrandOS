# Demand: what creators, SMMs and brands say they need (2026-10-23)

First `demand` run. Weighted to crypto/web3 creators and KOLs on X. Signals from the last
12 months unless marked **older**.

## The answer (5 lines max)

1. The loudest, most repeated pain is **Get trusted**: brands can't tell real from botted, and crypto companies hire KOL managers (est. $39k–$80k in the posts that say it outright) to check "real engagement, bots in the followers".
2. **Get found** in crypto got worse this year: X banned pay-to-post apps on Jan 15, Kaito killed Yaps (the leaderboard where small accounts got noticed), and small creators say Kaito Studio isn't built for them.
3. **Big-platform risk is live:** X announced **Creator Connect** in May 2026 (reported May 18 as "in development"; no launch confirmed since), AI matching of brands to "smaller and more niche" X creators. Creators can't apply, can't see it, and brands get in through an X sales rep.
4. Brands are the scarce side. More creators want deals than brands offer them, and mainstream creator budgets barely touch X. Crypto is where X creator money is.
5. Do: write phase 1 posts on "the leaderboards are gone, how do projects find you now?" and "x picks creators for brands now, and you can't see if you're in it". Start brand DMs before Nov 9.

## Findings

Every X quote below was read from the search index; x.com blocks direct page reads (HTTP 402),
so X post dates are worked out from the post ID and are approximate. Reddit is blocked entirely

### 1. Get trusted: the strongest, most repeated job (high confidence)

**Brand / buyer side**

- People paid to vet KOLs say vetting is the job. A YJVC KOL/BD Manager post ($68k–$80k est., ~5 months old) wants someone who already has "a database of dozens of vetted KOLs" and who will "check real engagement, bots in the followers, and conversion into actions" ([web3.career](https://web3.career/web3-kol-key-opinion-leader-bd-manager-yjvc/149465)). Flight3's KOL Lead role: "Source, vet, and onboard new KOLs through a rigorous process - checking authenticity, engagement quality" ([cryptocurrencyjobs.co](https://cryptocurrencyjobs.co/marketing/flight3-kol-lead-manager/), closed). Trust Wallet: "Identify, vet, onboard, and oversee" KOLs, $39k–$45k est. ([web3.career](https://web3.career/kol-manager-web3-trustwallet/104746)). High confidence: three employers, same wording.
- The KOL/influencer job board on web3.career listed 28 roles on Oct 23, 2026, with salary bands from $36k (Rarible) to $104k–$144k (Tether), plus Consensys ($90k–$109k), Bitmex (4 roles, $84k–$112k), Bitpanda, Woo, CoinMarketCap ([web3.career](https://web3.career/kol+remote-jobs)). **Inference:** crypto companies already spend a salary on the work the Index does for free. That's the brand-side price anchor.
- Brands on marketplace review sites, this year:
  - Upfluence reviewer (Jun 4, 2026, paying ~$700/mo): creators "seem to be primarily botted" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/upfluence.com)).
  - Insense advertiser (Sep 5, 2026): "people misrepresenting their languages and misrepresenting the followers" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/insense.pro)).
  - Collabstr reviewer (Sep 22, 2026): the creators' Instagram owners "never signed up for this website" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/collabstr.com)).
- Crypto marketers on X: @CristinCulver (~Sep 2026) on launches with 10M views: "Just explained this fake engagement launch slop to someone else" ([x.com](https://x.com/CristinCulver/status/2094872307990962409)). @jaxxdwyer (**older**, ~Mar 2025): "Costs are skyrocketing. ➜ Most engagements are fake." ([x.com](https://x.com/jaxxdwyer/status/1904896181979013623)).
- A creator says it out loud: @ZoraWeb3 (**older**, ~Aug 31, 2025, 9k followers): "If I started buying followers (say 100 - 200 a day until I added 11k more), no one would even notice." ([x.com](https://x.com/ZoraWeb3/status/1962477618290135416)). The rest of the post (search summary) says brands would then pitch them as a "small KOL" and marketing managers "still can't tell real engagement from fake". This is the single best Problem-post quote found.
- Survey data (medium confidence, one source each):
  - Influencer Marketing Hub 2026 benchmark (May 4, 2026, 600+ respondents): fake engagement/authenticity is 12.73% of challenge picks, second only to creator costs. Fake or bot followers are 56.5% of reported fraud/quality issues ([IMH](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report/)).
  - ContentGrip (Jun 29, 2026) reports a WFA study: 81% of 1,400 senior marketers met influencer fraud in 12 months ([ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/the-state-of-b2b-influencer-marketing/)). I couldn't find WFA's own report, and aggregators disagree on the dollar loss. Low confidence until the primary source is found.
- X itself is fighting fake engagement in crypto. It sued two users on Sep 17, 2026 over nine fake bitcoin-news accounts that allegedly took £207,000+ from the creator fund through "coordinated likes, replies and reposts" ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/21/x-sues-its-won-users-for-running-a-fake-bitcoin-news-bot-farm)). On Apr 9, 2026, Nikita Bier posted "Currently identifying and suspending 208 bots per minute and growing" ([TradingView/U.Today](https://www.tradingview.com/news/u_today:6b91f99fe094b:0-208-bots-per-minute-banned-will-crypto-x-be-purged/)).

**Creator side (creators who can't prove they're real)**

- Insense creator (Sep 29, 2026): rejected "because apparently the 'quality' of my account did not meet their standards" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/insense.pro)). The creator got a verdict with no reasons, which is the gap a visible brand read fills.

**Trust runs both ways (creators can't trust projects)**

- Influence360's survey of 143 Web3 KOLs (May 25, 2026, vendor study) found only 35% say every project they've worked with paid them ([TradingView/Cointelegraph](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:ce0ee2eea094b:0-influence360-launches-as-the-first-ai-data-driven-web3-kol-platform-with-global-kol-coverage-and-real-attribution/)). Medium confidence.
- @JoestarCrypto (~Mar 2026): "most KOL agencies will just make you shill the worst deals", so they now deal with teams directly ([x.com](https://x.com/JoestarCrypto/status/2030396823286784172)).
- **Inference:** this supports the parked "track record / receipts" idea, with a two-sided record: did the creator deliver, and did the project pay.

### 2. Get found: real, and crypto-specific this year (medium-high confidence)

- **The crypto discovery channel was removed.** On Jan 15, 2026, X revoked API access for apps that reward users for posting. Kaito sunset Yaps the same day and moved to Kaito Studio, "brand-vetted creator partnerships" ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/02/04/infofi-rise-crash-kaito-cookie-x-ban-information-finance/), [CoinDesk headline](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/01/15/kaito-to-sunset-yaps-as-x-cracks-down-on-infofi-apps-token-falls-17)). Cookie DAO also shut Snaps. High confidence: multiple outlets.
- In Kaito Studio, Kaito decides who gets in on "follower count, social reach, and impression count", and "Kaito is not obligated to disclose reasons for rejection". Brands pick creators "at their sole discretion" ([CoinGecko, updated May 7, 2026](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/what-is-kaito-earn-yap-points)).
- Small crypto creators on this:
  - @xzkevs (Oct 10, 2026): "there's not much to do here if you don't have a gig". The post also says Kaito Studio isn't friendly to small accounts and that buying views looks like the way ahead ([KuCoin community mirror](https://www.kucoin.com/news/community/SOL/6ac9a3a138a2640007936f03)).
  - Critic Erequendi (early 2026): Kaito Studio "looks more like a closed marketing agency where rewards may keep going to the same group of creators" ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/02/04/infofi-rise-crash-kaito-cookie-x-ban-information-finance/)).
  - **Older** (Aug 11, 2025), when Kaito raised its thresholds: @Telloo55: "What made Kaito famous and trendy in the first place was small accounts winning". @evans1vn: "this new update removed majority of small accounts from leaderboard". @Mr_CryptoYT: "try to make kaito for everyone" ([Blocmates](https://www.blocmates.com/news-posts/kaitofail-new-rules-ignite-war-between-big-and-small-web3-accounts)).
- General creators:
  - Insense creators: "it can be frustrating to not hear back from the majority of brands you apply to" (Jan 20, 2026). "I have not heard back from any of them." (Sep 28, 2026) ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/insense.pro)).
  - @mws (~Jul 2026): "Big creators get brands reaching out first But as a small creator, that" [last word of the indexed text] ([x.com](https://x.com/mws/status/2078176108915093633)).
- **Counter-signals (read these):**

### 3. Find the right creator (medium confidence)

- Agencies are working with more creators per campaign. Creo's president: "we're probably working with 30 or 40% more influencers on average per campaign". Later's CEO: "More and more brands want to work with more creators" ([Digiday, Mar 30, 2026](https://digiday.com/marketing/ai-influencer-discovery-tools-are-changing-how-agencies-cast-creators/)). Per the same piece, Walmart picks creators by engagement, not follower count.
- B2B marketers rank "finding and qualifying the right influencers" as their most-cited difficulty (TopRank 2025, via [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/the-state-of-b2b-influencer-marketing/)).
- Crypto audience overlap: @Evan_Mann (~Apr 2026): "most crypto KOL marketing feels like failure" ([x.com](https://x.com/Evan_Mann/status/2041919201795858485)). The search summary says the post argues that paying several KOLs for one launch hits the same audience again and again.
- KOL score vendors now sell "who follows you", not "how many". TwitterScore (~Sep 30, 2026): "Read the lists, not the counts" ([x.com](https://x.com/Twiter_score/status/2105689521207890042)). This is a vendor's claim, so low confidence as a demand signal, but it's the same message as BrandOS's Sat 24 post.

## So what for BrandOS

Phase: **1. Name it (Oct 23–Nov 1)**, 50% Problem, 50% Proof, sell nothing.

**Problem posts (strongest first, each backed by sourced facts above)**

1. **"the leaderboards are gone. so how do projects find you now?"** X killed pay-to-post apps on Jan 15. Kaito Yaps died the same day. Kaito Studio picks creators on follower count and reach, and doesn't have to say why it rejects you. Ends with: "how did the last project that paid you find you?" This is the crypto version of "invisible to brands", and it's this year's news.
2. **"x now picks creators for brands. you can't apply, and you can't see if you were picked."** Creator Connect, announced May 2026. Frame it as a fact, not an attack: matching happens in a room you can't see. Pairs with the Oct 23 thread "brands aren't ignoring you. they can't see you."
3. **"someone with 9k followers said: if i bought 11k more, no one would notice."** Paraphrase @ZoraWeb3, or quote with permission. Then the turn: the honest creator pays for this, because brands discount everyone. Fits Sat 24 ("follower count is the worst way to pick a creator").
4. **"x suspended 208 bots a minute in april. how many of your followers were real?"** Keep it as a question. This is the hook for the parked /bots check and the Verified badge later. Don't promise numbers BrandOS can't produce yet.
5. **"crypto companies pay a KOL manager a salary to answer one question about you: is this account real?"** Uses the YJVC / Trust Wallet / Flight3 posts. Don't use the $144k Tether figure: that listing isn't shown to be about vetting. Good for Fri 30 ("the most underpaid creators are the ones brands can't verify").

**Proof posts**

- Teardowns: show what a KOL manager checks, in their own job-post words: "real engagement, bots in the followers, and conversion into actions". Then show BrandOS's read of the same account. That turns "what a brand sees" into a checklist people already recognise.
- For the Mon 26 post ("i asked {n} brand marketers"): the public data says engagement beats follower count (Walmart), vetting is the #1 B2B difficulty, and fake followers are 56.5% of fraud issues. Use Bawsa's real DM answers first. These public numbers only back them up.

**DM targets**

- Brand side: the companies hiring KOL managers right now (Tether, Consensys, Bitmex, Bitpanda, Woo, CoinMarketCap on web3.career) have a named person whose job is vetting. Their KOL/influencer leads are the "how do you pick creators?" DMs for today.
- Creator side: ex-Kaito leaderboard regulars with 5k–100k followers, who lost their discovery channel in January. They match the plan's DM profile exactly.

**Index features the evidence asks for**

- Show *why*, not just a score. Insense and Kaito both reject creators without reasons, and creators resent it. A visible brand read is the difference.
- "Read the lists, not the counts": brands and vendors are moving to follower quality. An audience-quality line on each Index card (when the /bots check comes back) answers the #1 job.
- Later (receipts): a two-sided track record. Only 35% of KOLs say every project paid them, so "did this brand pay" is as wanted as "did this creator deliver".

**Pricing evidence**

- Brand side: a KOL manager salary is $39k–$144k a year, and Upfluence reviewers pay ~$700/mo. Creator side: KOLs earn $1k–$5k per campaign (survey), so $29/mo is under 3% of one campaign. Use that once, in phase 3 price posts, not now.

## Challenges a decision

**Decision: "Free to be listed. Pro to stand out", with "brands that viewed you" as the strongest upgrade trigger, and the brand side opening Nov 9.**

The evidence says brands, not creators, are the scarce side:

- More creators want deals than brands offer ([@lauren_huttner](https://x.com/lauren_huttner/status/1950228898220036121)).
- Mainstream brand creator budgets barely include X ([Digiday/CreatorIQ](https://digiday.com/marketing/in-graphic-detail-heres-what-the-creator-economy-is-expected-to-look-like-in-2026/)).
- 82+ crypto projects have shut down this year ([Cointelegraph](https://x.com/Cointelegraph/status/2112660380757418442)).
- X now offers large advertisers managed creator matching ([NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/x-unveils-creator-connect-an-ai-matchmaking-tool-for-brand-creator-campaigns/)).

If few brands browse, "brands that viewed you" shows zeros, and the main Pro reason is empty when the price posts start (Nov 9). The evidence points to starting the 5-a-day brand DMs earlier than Nov 9. Bawsa's call.

**Decision: the problem we own ("creators are invisible to brands").** It still holds, and the evidence for it is strong. But X shipped a product aimed at the same problem on X in May. BrandOS's room to work in is what Creator Connect doesn't do:

- creators opt in and can see themselves;
- the brand read behind each entry is public;
- brands too small to have an X client partner (most crypto projects) can browse.

That's positioning, not a reversal.
